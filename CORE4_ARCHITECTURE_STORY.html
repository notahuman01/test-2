Cycle16 Architecture

Core-4 Architecture Story

The current shared Charades-STA and ActivityNet Captions recipe is built around four active zero-shot modules: Cohen's d scoring, DA-NMS voting, confidence-contrast onset weighting, and modern dynamic refinement.

Source: experiments/cycle16/CORE4_ARCHITECTURE_STORY.md

1 Cohen's d scoring

Score proposals as standardized inside/outside separation.

2 DA-NMS voting

Select proposals by density-aware temporal consensus.

3 Onset weighting

Amplify rising, locally confident query evidence.

4 Modern refinement

Correct start and end boundaries after candidate selection.

Important caveat: this is the design story order, not a claim that the additive ladder is strictly monotonic. The evidence says the modules interact; the complete stack is what produces the headline result.

Module 1: Cohen's d Scoring

The first conceptual shift is to stop treating proposal quality as only an inner-minus-outer raw contrast. Cohen's d asks a more stable question:

Is the proposal's inside region separated from its surrounding context by a meaningful standardized effect size?

Instead of only rewarding high inside activation, it normalizes the inside/outside gap by variation. This matters because TAG-style proposal curves are noisy: a large raw difference can be less reliable than a smaller but cleaner separation.

Conceptually:

$$ S_p = \frac{\mu_{inside}(p) - \mu_{outside}(p)} {\sigma_{pooled}(p) + \epsilon} $$

Why it belongs in the final architecture:

  • It makes proposal scoring more scale-aware.
  • It helps ActivityNet strongly when paired with DA-NMS.
  • It gives a clean statistical interpretation: proposal confidence is an effect size, not just a raw gap.
scoring_type: cohens_d

Module 2: DA-NMS Voting

The second conceptual shift is to make final proposal selection density-aware. Plain Soft-NMS suppresses or keeps proposals mostly through overlap and score. DA-NMS changes the vote into a support-weighted consensus:

$$ \hat{S}_j = \sum_i IoU(p_i, p_j)^\gamma S_i D_i^{\alpha_D} $$

where \(D_i\) is an internal proposal-density term. Dense proposal neighborhoods get more voting support; isolated overly-wide spans lose influence.

Why it belongs after Cohen's d in the story:

  • Cohen's d improves the quality of the proposal score \(S_i\).
  • DA-NMS then asks which scored proposals are supported by nearby alternatives.
  • The pair turns scoring from "pick the best isolated span" into "pick the best statistically separated span that has local temporal support."
voting_method: da_nms
gamma: 0.9   # ActivityNet
gamma: 0.2   # Charades
window_radius: 8

Module 3: Confidence-Contrast Onset Weighting

The third module sharpens the timeline before proposal scoring and voting. It focuses on rising evidence:

$$ g_i^+ = \max(s_i - s_{i-1}, 0) $$

Then it gates the rise by confidence contrast:

$$ s_i' = s_i \left(1 + \beta \cdot g_i^+ \cdot h_i^{conf-contrast}\right) $$

This says: do not amplify every frame; amplify frames where the query evidence is rising and locally confident.

Why it belongs after scoring/voting in the story:

  • Cohen's d and DA-NMS define how proposals are evaluated and selected.
  • Onset weighting improves the signal those modules consume.
  • It is especially important for Charades, where short human actions often start sharply.

Module 4: Modern Dynamic Refinement

The final module fixes boundaries after proposal scoring and voting have produced a strong candidate. Older v00 start refinement was a narrow start-shift compatibility module. The modern version is cleaner: it refines both start and end using a shared dynamic policy.

v00_start_refinement_compat: false

dynamic_refinement_mode: both
dynamic_refinement_policy: legacy
dynamic_refinement_start_behavior: expand_only
dynamic_refinement_end_behavior: expand_only
dynamic_refinement_ths: 0.002
dynamic_refinement_max_end_ratio: 0.35

Why it is last in the story:

  • Cohen's d defines candidate quality.
  • DA-NMS chooses candidates by consensus.
  • Onset improves the temporal evidence.
  • Modern refinement performs the final boundary correction once the right proposal family has surfaced.

This keeps the architecture mathematically cleaner than stacking the old v00 start-shift module with modern start/end refinement.

Why This Order Makes Sense

Cohen's d scoring -> DA-NMS voting -> onset -> modern refinement
  1. First, define a better proposal score.
  2. Then, define a better voting/selection mechanism for those scores.
  3. Then, sharpen the timeline signal feeding the scorer and voter.
  4. Finally, refine boundaries after the strongest candidate has emerged.

Short Claim Version

Cycle16 upgrades TAG with four active zero-shot modules:

Cohen's d scoring + DA-NMS voting + confidence-contrast onset + modern dynamic refinement

The recipe keeps the TAG hard-token spine and does not use LLMs or training. The gain comes from replacing raw proposal contrast and plain consensus with an effect-size scorer, density-aware voting, onset-gated temporal evidence, and a modern boundary refiner.
